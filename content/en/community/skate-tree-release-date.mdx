## Reading Community Reports Without Getting Fooled

Community reports are genuinely valuable — they're often the first sign that something is moving. The trick is classifying them correctly instead of treating every post as news.

| Claim You'll See | Why It's a Red Flag | How to Verify |
|---|---|---|
| "Date confirmed!" with no link | No primary source attached | Search the official channels |
| Screenshot of a store page | Easy to edit, easy to misread | Open the live store page yourself |
| "My friend is a tester" | Unverifiable by design | Wait for public playtest news |
| Countdown timer on a fan site | Fan-made, not official | Check the developer's own channels |
| "It launches with the next update" | Assumes a shared patch schedule | Look for an official roadmap |
| Old trailer reposted as new | Recycled content farms engagement | Check the upload date |

Player experience is worth reading for feel and performance — how the controls handle, whether the frame rate holds, what the trick system rewards. It is not worth reading for dates.

### The One Rule That Saves You Every Time

Ask: *who benefits if I believe this?* An official announcement benefits the developer by building hype. A fake date benefits a content farm by farming clicks. Once you frame it that way, sorting signal from noise becomes almost automatic.
